# Nightshade 7.1.0 — Observatory

## Summary

Every screen rebuilt on one design system, and a hard pass over the things that
went wrong on the rig.

7.0 gave you the Darkroom. 7.1 is about the hours before it: the interface you
drive the night through, and the parts of the imaging chain that nights on the
rig found broken. The whole app now sits on the **Observatory** kit — one
set of panels, wells, readouts, fields and buttons, one header on every page,
one rail — instead of the eleven-screen patchwork it grew into.

Underneath that, three new pieces of instrumentation: a **Sequencer ledger**
that shows a long run as a ledger instead of an endless tree, **DepthLock**,
which measures how deep a region of your target actually is and tells you what
it still needs, and **focuser backlash calibration**, which measures your
gear train instead of asking you to guess at it.

The rig fixes matter more than any of it. A sequence that died 1.5 seconds
after slewing, a polar alignment that failed every time you ran it westward,
an app that froze solid on the first successful plate solve, and a guider
failure that kept exposing and then parked an attended mount — all four are
fixed here, and all four were invisible to the simulator.

Those nights are real hardware evidence; the fixes they produced have not
themselves been flown. See **What's verified**.

## Observatory

The redesign covers every screen, not a subset.

- **One kit, one grammar.** Panels, wells, readouts, form rows, fields,
  buttons, underline tabs, chips, banners, side panels and the night band all
  come from `nightshade_ui` now. A control looks and behaves the same whether
  it is in Settings, Guiding or the Flat wizard.
- **One page header everywhere**, with the title yielding to the actions on a
  phone rather than truncating them.
- **The Observatory rail** replaces the old navigation, with a bottom nav that
  stays on screen below 768 px instead of sliding off it.
- **Tonight** (`/dashboard`), **Plan**, **Analytics**, **Weather**,
  **Equipment**, **Guiding**, **Settings**, **Onboarding** and the **Darkroom**
  were each rebuilt on the sheet rather than re-skinned. Weather is now a
  full-bleed radar with a glass HUD; the Darkroom makes the picture the screen
  and the containers panels; Analytics puts its charts in wells and gives every
  icon button a tooltip.
- **The select has its own popover** instead of Material's menu, which is what
  was opening menus mid-screen instead of on the button that spawned them.
- **Sentence case throughout**, including composed sentences and their
  localisation twins.
- **Red night** reaches the Imaging canvas and the Weather map, which were both
  painting off the red axis.

## The Sequencer ledger

A twelve-hour sequence used to be a tree you scrolled. Now:

- **Three density modes** — a persisted preference, switched from the canvas
  bar — from the full tree down to a ledger of aligned columns with collapsed
  rollups.
- **Contiguous runs fold into one row**, so forty identical exposures read as
  one line with a count instead of forty.
- **Sticky ancestors and a gutter map**: the branch you are running stays
  pinned at the top while you scroll, and the gutter shows where you are in the
  whole run.
- **The node inspector gained Settings, Activity and Notes tabs**, and
  switching tabs no longer scrolls the screen pager underneath it.
- **The toolbox Queue tab is now Targets**, showing the sequence's own targets
  alongside a saved-for-later wishlist.
- **Pre-flight refuses a centring sequence when no plate solver is configured**
  rather than discovering it mid-run.

## DepthLock

Mark the structure you care about. Nightshade tracks what it still needs.

Draw a box over the detail and a second box over nearby blank sky, pick a
depth, and every matching exposure from then on measures that patch — across
nights and across sessions. A goal is one filter, on one target, with one
setup.

- **Done is defined by the data, not the clock.** Six hours under a bright moon
  and six hours on a transparent night are not the same data. The goal finishes
  when the structure reaches the depth you asked for.
- **It allocates clear sky between filters.** Once each filter's goal can say
  what it still needs — *Ha · done · OIII · 3.4 h · SII · 1.1 h* — you can
  spend tonight on the one that is behind. A Smart Exposure plan reads those
  numbers directly.
- **It gives the negative answer too.** When the calibration error floor caps
  how deep a region can go, more hours will not move it. DepthLock says so,
  names the ceiling, and names the two things that would actually help.

Full documentation is in `docs/depthlock.md`.

## Focuser backlash, measured

7.0 asked you for a backlash figure. Most people do not have one.

- **The calibration suite measures it**: two scans, one approaching from below
  and one from above, fitted and differenced, with the result persisted per
  focuser and a wizard that states what the run will cost before it starts.
- **The backlash model was wrong**, found by simulating a gear train, and is
  corrected here along with the reversal-budget formula the code actually uses.
- **A cancelled calibration reports as cancelled**, not as a failure.
- **The shipped default is now zero**, because only the operator knows theirs —
  an invented 350 was reaching both the settings field and the autofocus
  endpoint.

Related autofocus fixes: best focus now lands from below and is verified with a
real frame; the MAD outlier filter was deleting the focus region on clean
sweeps; a symmetric model now gets a symmetric sample; and the star count
reports what was detected rather than the brightest-N cap.

## What the rig taught us

Four of these were invisible to the simulator and cost real nights.

- **The slew validator killed every sequence 1.5 seconds after it started.** It
  compared a J2000 target against an of-date read-back from the mount. Arrival
  is now validated in the mount's own coordinate frame.
- **Polar alignment failed every westward run.** The rotation-axis fit picked
  the antipode, so the correction pointed the wrong way. The fit is now
  hemisphere-consistent, Dec is re-read per step, and TPPA frames are solved
  with a hint from the mount on a time budget that fits between steps rather
  than a blind 30-second solve. Use a 30° step and bin 2.
- **The app froze solid on the first successful plate solve.** The annotation
  pipeline was reading the 896 MB GLADE+ catalogue line by line on the UI
  isolate. It is off the UI isolate now, the star matcher is bucketed instead
  of quadratic, and an image texture leak went with it.
- **A guider failure kept exposing, and then parked an attended mount.** The
  imaging train is now arbitrated — exposures stop when the guider is dead —
  and parking is an explicit opt-in (`parkAndCloseWhenRecoveryGivesUp`) rather
  than something recovery decides on a guess.
- **A failed INDI reader recovery wedged the client out of ever recovering.**
  One failed reconnect left the reader marked `Restarting` forever, so the
  heartbeat never tried again and an INDI server that came back a minute later
  stayed dead to the app. Found by the integration for this release.
- **ASCOM mount site and time now route through the COM worker**, not the
  `RwLock` guard, and the app asks which way to reconcile site and time on
  connect instead of picking one.
- **Thumbnails no longer decode full-resolution FITS on the UI isolate.** The
  sidecars are read first, the decode is cell-sized, and the frame is sampled
  rather than fully converted.

## Also in 7.1

- **Camera sensor specs have one resolution chain.** Framing states which tier
  its geometry came from, an override claims only what you actually changed,
  and the planner says a missing spec once instead of three times. The planner's
  fuzzy camera matcher was returning the wrong sensor.
- **Location**: tiered Wi-Fi positioning with per-tier consent and a stated
  accuracy radius, and Detect location falls back to an internet lookup in one
  click.
- **The Profiles tab edits the profile you click** — it had no detail pane at
  all — and the profile menu opens on its button rather than mid-screen.
- **Recovery and session reporting headline the cause** rather than the last
  thing that failed during teardown, and the reporter finds the catalog next to
  the solver.
- **Performance**: the mount position poll no longer repaints the whole window,
  the status bar's selection is scoped, the dashboard has repaint boundaries,
  and the frame-timing probe reports UI-isolate block time and image-cache
  state.
- **The launch-time catalog modal is gone**; the Tonight checklist owns that
  now.
- **LX200**: the NYX answers `:GG#` with `+05:00`, not `+05`.

## Upgrading

- **The desktop, mobile and OTA versions move together** to 7.1.0+28. The
  release build fails loudly if they ever disagree.
- **Masters and sidecars written by 6.2.0 are still void.** The normalization
  noise-fit bug shipped in that build; v1 sidecars are refused at
  `MASTER_STATE_VERSION` 2. Re-stack anything made with 6.2.0.
- **Check your focuser backlash setting.** The shipped default is now zero. If
  you were relying on the old invented 350, run the calibration wizard instead
  of re-entering it.
- **Weather safety remains fail-closed by default.** With no weather device and
  an API that has never been fetched, an unattended rig will park.

## What's verified

This is the release's scoped capability claim. It appears word for word in
the README's "What is actually verified" section and in
`docs/known-limitations.md`.

> **Nightshade has now been run under a real sky. The fixes in 7.1.0 have not.** Two sessions on the owner's Windows rig — 13 and 14 September 2026, against a Pegasus NYX-101 mount, a ZWO camera, a ZWO EAF focuser and a guided imaging train — are where most of this release's imaging-chain work comes from. Those nights establish that the mount was commanded to move and moved, that frames were taken under a real sky, that plate solving was independently verified to 0.17 arcsec, and that backlash on that EAF measures about 105 steps. They equally establish that **both nights were lost**: a sequence died 1.5 s after slewing, polar alignment failed every westward run, the app froze on its first successful plate solve, and a guider failure kept exposing and then parked an attended mount. Every one of those defects is fixed in this release, and **every one of those fixes was written after the rig went home** — each is reproduced from a rig log or a simulator fault injection and covered by tests, and **none of them has been flown**. One measurement from those nights is still unexplained: the mount moved 12.1 degrees for a 10 degree commanded step. Fully-unattended **headless** acquisition and a full-night soak are **not** verified and must be supervised. **Linux** is where this release's suites and gates were run; the **Windows** and **Android** artifacts are built by CI and were not installed or exercised for this candidate; **macOS** and **iOS** are unbuilt and ship no artifact. Every other device path — switches, domes, covers, and the remaining vendor SDKs — is **capability-gated**: present in the app, but not a support guarantee until it is verified on your rig.

## Known limitations

- **On-sky validation of this build is owed.** The rig fixes above were each
  reproduced from a live-rig log or a simulator fault injection and are covered
  by tests, but this tree has not been flown as a whole. The polar-alignment
  and slew-frame fixes in particular deserve a night before you trust an
  unattended run to them.
- **One open TPPA question**: on 2026-09-13 the mount moved 12.1° for a 10°
  commanded step. That is not explained yet, and it is not a software fix in
  this release.
- **Windows and Android runtime validation is owed.** Both binaries are built
  by CI and published here, but neither was installed or exercised for this
  release, and the Windows goldens were not re-captured.
- **The focuser backlash calibration has not run against a real focuser in this
  build.** The model was corrected against a simulated gear train; an EAF
  measured 105 steps at position 6620 on an earlier build.
- **DepthLock has not been run across real nights.** Its forecast, yield,
  allocation and curve paths pass against seeded data; the multi-night ledger
  has not accumulated a real target.
- **SFTP password auth is deliberately absent** pending a dependency decision.
- The curves operation renders read-only in this build's editor.
- `denoise` on very large RGB masters has a high peak-memory profile.
- With no observing site configured, the default altitude trigger skips
  targets; the run outcome says so.
